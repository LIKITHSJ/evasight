# 5. Attack method

The attack works in the problem space. It changes the packets the attacker controls, works out how each feature of the flow changes as a result, and then checks that the new flow is one the flow meter could have produced. We never change a feature value on its own.

For the CIC datasets, padding adds p bytes to each forward data packet, where p is at most ε times the mean payload of a data packet in that flow and is capped so that no packet exceeds the 1,460-byte maximum segment size. Packets without payload, such as SYN and ACK packets, are not padded. The total forward bytes grow by p times the number of data packets, the maximum forward packet length grows by p, and the minimum grows by p only when every forward packet carries payload. Delay adds t to every gap between forward packets, where t is at most ε times the mean forward gap. The mean, minimum and maximum forward gap all grow by t, and the total by t times the number of gaps. Delay is capped twice. The flow must stay within the 120 s flow timeout, and no gap may cross the 5 s activity timeout. Because only the smallest and largest gap of a flow are recorded, we allow delay only when all gaps are below the threshold (and then only up to it) or all are above it. Padding is rounded down to whole bytes and delay to whole microseconds.

Features that depend on these values are then recomputed. Flow duration grows by the growth of the forward span. Byte and packet rates, the forward segment size, the overall maximum and minimum packet length and the mean packet length follow exactly from their definitions. Two features need an assumption, which we state openly. The average packet size of the improved CIC-IDS2017 release contains a small per-flow residual that the other features do not explain, and we keep this residual constant. The forward subflow byte count does not equal the total forward bytes in that release, so we scale it in proportion to them. Backward features are not changed. Flows with any forward bulk activity are left unperturbed, because the bulk features depend on the timing of individual packets.

For UNSW-NB15 we first measured how the Argus features relate to each other. The packet rate equals the number of packets minus one divided by the duration for 99.70% of flows; the source and destination loads equal bytes times eight times (packets minus one) over packets, divided by the duration, for 99% and 99.94% of flows; and the mean source packet size equals the source bytes divided by the source packets, rounded, for 99.53%. Padding adds p bytes, at most ε times the mean source packet size, to each payload packet. Because UNSW-NB15 does not count payload packets, we treat all packets of a non-TCP flow as payload packets and all but three of a TCP flow, leaving out the SYN, the handshake ACK and the FIN. Delay adds t to each gap between source packets, at most ε times the mean source gap, so the duration grows by t times the number of gaps and the mean source gap by t. The packet rate and both loads are rescaled by the ratio of old to new duration rather than recomputed, which is exact where the formulas hold and keeps the small deviations of the other flows. When the TCP handshake was observed, the delayed handshake ACK also raises the ACK delay and the round-trip time by t. Jitter does not change, because every gap moves by the same amount.

A perturbed flow is accepted only if it breaks no validity rule that the original flow satisfied. The rules cover non-negative counts, consistent minima, means and maxima, totals that equal means times counts, the flow timeouts and the packet size limits. We kept a rule only if clean training traffic satisfies it: rules that held only for TCP were limited to TCP, and a timing rule for UNSW-NB15 was dropped because 0.7% of clean flows broke it.

The search is a grid over the two actions. For each attack flow we try padding and delay levels of 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1 times the budget, 25 combinations in all, and keep the valid candidate with the lowest attack score. A grid is enough because there are only two actions and tree ensembles give no useful gradient. Budgets are run in increasing order and a flow keeps its best result from any smaller budget, so the evasion rate can only rise with ε. We report evasion as the share of attack flows the model detected before the attack (score at least 0.5) whose score falls below 0.5 afterwards. Flows the model already missed are not counted as evasions. The attack is deterministic, and running it again reproduces every number exactly.
