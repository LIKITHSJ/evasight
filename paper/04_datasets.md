# 4. Datasets and data quality

We use three public datasets. Two come from the same flow meter, which lets us compare a cleaned and an uncleaned release, and the third uses a different tool and feature set.

CIC-IDS2017 is used in the improved release by Engelen et al., which regenerated the flows from the original packet captures with a corrected version of CICFlowMeter and relabelled them. After removing 5 rows with non-finite values we keep 2,099,971 flows from five capture days. CSE-CIC-IDS2018 is used in its original release, because no corrected release produced with CICFlowMeter exists; the available alternatives either use a different flow meter or publish normalised values, which breaks the physical relationships our attack relies on. Of its 16,233,002 rows we remove 59 repeated header lines and 95,760 rows with non-finite values. To fit the data into 8 GB of memory we keep all attack flows and a seeded 10% sample of benign flows, which raises the attack share from 17.0% to 54.3%. We report this share rather than treat it as the natural one. UNSW-NB15 is used through its official training and testing sets, 257,673 flows in total, described by 42 features from Argus and Zeek.

All splits follow the same rules. We drop feature vectors that appear with conflicting labels, remove duplicate feature vectors, and make sure no feature vector appears in more than one partition. For the two CIC datasets we split each class separately by time into 70% training, 15% validation and 15% test, and remove the flows that lie just before each cut (within 60 s, and at most 1% of the class) so that bursts of near-identical flows cannot cross a boundary. In CIC-IDS2017 we also drop the 11,979 flows labelled as attempted attacks. This gives 1,270,642, 267,435 and 274,132 flows for CIC-IDS2017 and 1,692,819, 352,008 and 366,838 for CSE-CIC-IDS2018. UNSW-NB15 has no timestamps, so we keep its official test set and use a 15% subset of the official training set, chosen by hashing the feature vector, as validation. When the same record appears in both official sets we keep the training copy, which leaves 107,740 training and 55,005 test records before our own cleaning and 85,649, 14,981 and 52,640 after it.

We exclude three kinds of columns from the model input. Identifiers such as addresses, ports of the client and flow IDs are removed. Metadata such as the capture day is kept for splitting but never used as a feature, because in the CIC data each attack ran on a particular day and the day alone would reveal the label. Finally, we exclude features whose values we could not verify: the forward and backward header lengths, which contain negative values, and the TCP flow time of the improved CIC-IDS2017 release, whose definition we could not confirm. The models therefore use 79, 68 and 42 features.

Checking the data against our attack constraints exposed four problems that, to our knowledge, are not documented. First, the timestamps of CSE-CIC-IDS2018 use a 12-hour clock without AM or PM, so afternoon traffic sorts before morning traffic. All hours lie between 1 and 12, and mapping hours 1 to 7 to the afternoon matches the published attack schedule exactly; for example, FTP brute force then falls between 10:33 and 11:58 and SSH brute force between 14:01 and 15:32 on 14 February. A time-based split built on the raw timestamps would mix the order of each day. Second, in the improved CIC-IDS2017 release every UDP and ICMP flow records header and segment sizes that only make sense for TCP, so 47% of clean flows broke two rules that hold for 99.9% of TCP flows; these rules must be limited to TCP. Third, the bulk-transfer features, which are zero throughout the original CIC-IDS2017 release, are non-zero in the improved one, and they depend on packet timing that cannot be recovered from flow summaries. Fourth, many duplicate and conflicting records remain: 47,026 CSE-CIC-IDS2018 flows, most of them labelled infiltration, share their feature vector with flows of the other label, and 829 conflicting and 8,646 duplicate records remain in UNSW-NB15 after the loader's own cleaning.
